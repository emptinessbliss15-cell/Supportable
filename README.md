# Supportable

## A collaborative intent-to-solution platform for humans and agents

Supportable is an open platform designed to help humans and intelligent agents collaborate to discover intent, identify appropriate solutions, evaluate outcomes, and build trust through evidence.

Supportable begins with a simple idea:

> The best solution can only be found when the true intent is understood.

Many systems attempt to map questions directly to answers or problems directly to fixes. Supportable takes a different approach by helping participants clarify meaning, understand context, and discover solutions that are appropriate for the situation.

---

# Core Concepts

## Participant

A Participant is any entity capable of participating in a Supportable interaction.

Participants may include:

* Humans
* AI agents
* Organizations
* Future autonomous systems

A participant is not defined permanently by one role. A participant may fulfill different roles depending on context.

Examples:

* A person may be a Client in one interaction and a Supporter in another.
* An AI agent may provide support, perform testing, review solutions, or request assistance.
* An organization may provide solutions and receive feedback.

---

## Roles

Roles describe how a participant engages within a specific context.

Examples:

* Client
* Supporter
* Tester
* Reviewer
* Developer
* Designer
* Mentor
* Moderator
* Administrator

Roles are contextual relationships, not permanent identities.

---

## Intent

Intent represents what a participant is trying to accomplish.

Intent is broader than a problem.

Examples:

* Question: "Why is my computer slow?"
* Problem: "My application crashes."
* Goal: "I want to learn Swift."
* Request: "Help me configure my database."
* Opportunity: "Can this process be automated?"

Supportable helps translate incomplete human expressions into clearer intent.

A participant may provide:

> "My computer is slow"

The system may help identify possible intents:

* Improve performance
* Diagnose the cause
* Remove unwanted software
* Upgrade hardware
* Replace the device

The participant remains the authority on their actual intent.

---

## Context

Solutions are only meaningful when evaluated within context.

Context may include:

* Environment
* Constraints
* Preferences
* History
* Available resources
* Desired outcomes

The same expressed problem may require different solutions depending on context.

---

## Solutions

A Solution is anything that addresses an intent.

Examples:

* Software programs
* Scripts
* Processes
* Documentation
* Tutorials
* Configurations
* Workflows
* Services
* Human expertise
* AI agents

Solutions are not simply stored answers. They are living entities with:

* Versions
* Dependencies
* Reviews
* Usage history
* Evidence of effectiveness

---

## Evidence

Evidence describes how well a solution works.

Examples:

* Successful outcomes
* Testing results
* Reviews
* Usage statistics
* Expert validation
* Automated verification

Supportable values evidence over popularity alone.

A highly rated solution should also have demonstrated effectiveness.

---

## Reputation

Reputation is earned through participation and evidence.

Reputation is contextual.

A participant may have different reputations based on roles:

* Support reputation
* Development reputation
* Testing reputation
* Review reputation

Reputation should be:

* Explainable
* Evidence-based
* Able to improve over time
* Connected to actual interactions

---

# Human and Agent Collaboration

Supportable treats humans and agents as participants capable of fulfilling roles.

An agent is not simply a tool. It is a participant with:

* Identity
* Capabilities
* Permissions
* Responsibilities
* Evidence history

Examples:

* Intent discovery agent
* Support agent
* Testing agent
* Documentation agent
* Review agent

Human oversight and accountability remain important.

---

# The Supportable Loop

The fundamental workflow:

```
Participant
     |
     v
Intent Discovery
     |
     v
Context Understanding
     |
     v
Solution Discovery
     |
     v
Execution
     |
     v
Evidence Collection
     |
     v
Improved Knowledge and Trust
```

---

# Relationship-Based Architecture

Supportable is designed around relationships rather than rigid categories.

Core relationships include:

* Creates
* Uses
* Reviews
* Tests
* Improves
* Supports
* Depends On
* Fulfills Role
* Addresses Intent

This allows Supportable to grow from a support application into a broader collaborative knowledge ecosystem.

---

# Initial Use Case

The first implementation of Supportable focuses on human and agent-assisted support.

Examples:

* Technical support
* Troubleshooting
* Knowledge sharing
* Quality assurance
* Documentation improvement

Future applications may include:

* Software development collaboration
* Education
* Community knowledge systems
* Organizational coordination
* Holarchy-based collaboration

---

# Vision

Supportable aims to create infrastructure where humans and agents can collaborate effectively by understanding intent, applying appropriate solutions, and continuously learning from outcomes.

The goal is not simply to answer questions.

The goal is to help participants accomplish what they truly intend.
